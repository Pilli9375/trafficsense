# Indian 2x2 scenario

A 2x2 grid of signalised intersections with an Indian vehicle mix, at two demand levels
(off-peak and peak), used to compare the baselines (FixedTime, Webster-tuned FixedTime and
MaxPressure) before the LLM controller is added.

**In short:** the network is generated with CityFlow's own grid generator (200 m spacing,
3.0 m lanes, 3 lanes per direction, 45 km/h). Vehicles are 45% two-wheelers, 18%
auto-rickshaws, 31% cars, 2% buses and 5% trucks, with per-type size and driving parameters.
Demand was calibrated against measured capacity under the tuned MaxPressure: off-peak
2375 veh/h (busiest lane at 0.11–0.13 of its saturation flow) and peak 4750 veh/h
(0.21–0.22; the network stops draining under MaxPressure at 5500 veh/h). By Webster's
controller-independent measure, peak uses only about 55% of the intersections' capacity.
MaxPressure is the best baseline off-peak; at peak a Webster-tuned fixed-time plan is best,
and 30 s FixedTime no longer drains. CityFlow is lane-based, so lane-free driving and
two-wheeler filtering are **not** modelled (next section).

Every table in this document is generated by `scripts/make_indian_tables.py` from
`outputs/indian_2x2/runs/*/summary.json`, `outputs/validation/indian_demand_calibration.json`
(and the earlier `indian_demand_calibration_maxpressure_5s.json`), the flow files and `configs/scenarios/indian_2x2.yaml`; `make_indian_tables.py --check` fails
if the document and the data disagree.

## What this scenario can and cannot represent

**CityFlow is a lane-based simulator, so this is not a model of real Indian traffic
behaviour.** In CityFlow every vehicle drives in exactly one lane, follows the vehicle in
front of it (car-following), and changes lane only at discrete moments (and here, not at all:
`lane_change: false`). It has no notion of lateral position inside a lane. It therefore
**cannot** model:

* **non-lane-disciplined driving**: vehicles straddling lanes, using the gaps between lanes,
  or forming more "lanes" than are marked;
* **two-wheelers filtering** between or alongside cars, or queuing two or three abreast at a
  stop line and moving to the front of the queue;
* **width-based interaction**: CityFlow reads a vehicle's `width` but does not use it in the
  dynamics (it is only written to the replay file; checked in CityFlow's source,
  `engine.cpp`/`vehicle.cpp`), so a 0.7 m motorcycle occupies a lane exactly like a 2.4 m bus;
* bicycles, pedestrians, buses stopping at kerbside stops, on-street parking, encroachment,
  U-turns, and wrong-way driving;
* per-type turning speed: CityFlow uses the same turn speed (8.33 m/s) for every vehicle.

What we do model is the **vehicle mix and per-type parameters**: each vehicle type has its own
length, minimum gap, desired time headway, maximum speed, and acceleration and deceleration.
This changes how much space a queue takes, how fast a queue discharges and how fast vehicles
accelerate, but motorcycles still queue in single file like cars. Because of this, the
saturation flows and capacities below are **CityFlow's** for this mix, and are likely
**lower in vehicles per hour** than the same road would carry with real Indian two-wheeler
behaviour. Results from this scenario should be described as "a lane-based simulation with
an Indian vehicle mix", not as a simulation of Indian traffic.

Two further simplifications:

* **Left-hand traffic is represented by mirroring.** India drives on the left; CityFlow's
  generator builds right-hand traffic. A square grid is mirror-symmetric, so a right-hand
  network is the mirror image of a left-hand one, and the simulation is the same with the
  turn labels swapped: CityFlow's `turn_right` (kerb side, allowed in every phase including
  the transition phase) plays the role of India's **free left turn**, and CityFlow's
  `turn_left` (crosses opposing traffic, protected phase) plays the role of India's **right
  turn**. Config, code and output use CityFlow's labels; this document says which is which.
* **Demand is synthetic**: Poisson arrivals with fixed turning probabilities, not counts from
  a real junction. The levels are calibrated against measured capacity (below), not against
  observed Indian traffic volumes.

## Demand

`scripts/generate_indian_flow.py --level <offpeak|peak> --seed <n>` writes
`data/scenarios/indian_2x2/flow_<level>_s<n>.json` and a `.meta.json` (vehicle type of every
flow entry, counts, settings). All randomness comes from one `numpy` generator seeded with
the seed, so different seeds give different traffic and the same seed always gives the same
file (tested).

* **Arrivals**: a Poisson process on each of the 8 entry roads over 0–3600 s, floored to whole
  seconds (CityFlow needs integer start times). Each vehicle is its own flow entry.
* **Entry weights** (ASSUMPTION): east–west is the major road (weight 1.0 on each W and E
  entry), north–south the minor road (0.6 on each N and S entry).
* **Turns** (ASSUMPTION), drawn at every intersection the vehicle reaches: straight 0.60,
  free left (CityFlow `turn_right`) 0.25, right (CityFlow `turn_left`) 0.15. A route that would
  come back to an intersection it already passed (circling the block) is redrawn; this shifts
  the realised turn shares by well under 1 percentage point (tested).
* **Vehicle type**: drawn per vehicle from the normalised shares above.

## Capacity and demand calibration

The goal was demand that is realistic for a signalised network: off-peak clearly
undersaturated, peak near capacity with heavy queues but still draining, judged under
MaxPressure. `scripts/calibrate_indian_demand.py` does this in three steps. The tables below
are from the re-calibration with the tuned MaxPressure (15 s decision interval); the first
calibration (5 s) is compared with it further down, and a controller-independent check
(Webster's Y) follows that.

### 1. Measured capacity

Measured with the vehicle mix (`trafficsense.sim.capacity`):

<!-- BEGIN:capacity -->
| Quantity (mixed traffic, measured) | Value | How |
|---|---|---|
| Saturation flow, straight lane | 2103 veh/h | queue discharge after 900 s red; slope of a line fitted to cumulative departures in green s 10–70; mean of 30 random-order queues |
| Saturation flow, protected-turn lane (Indian right turn) | 2024 veh/h | same |
| Start-up lost time (mean of both lanes) | 1.88 s | same fit: time at which the line crosses zero departures |
| Entry admission limit (CityFlow insertion) | 1341 veh/h | permanent green, fed 2 veh/s, minutes 20–60 |
<!-- END:capacity -->

The **saturation flow** is what a green phase can serve: it is the number a signal's capacity
is built on (capacity of a lane ≈ saturation flow × its share of green). The **entry admission
limit** is a CityFlow property: a waiting vehicle is put on an entry lane, at speed 0, only once
the previous vehicle is `length + minGap` ahead, so an entry lane cannot admit more than this
even under a permanent green (see also the correction in `docs/validation.md`, section 2).

The first calibration measured saturation flow by counting departures from one queue built
by periodic flows (2100 and 2040 veh/h). That queue always has the same regular order of
vehicle types, and the start-up lost time it gives depends strongly on which types lead
(3.3 to 5.7 s across the three flow files' mixes). It now comes from 30 queues in random
order, the same measurement the Webster controller uses (`docs/baselines.md`, section 2).
The saturation flows agree within 1%. Only the ANALYTIC indicators (busiest-lane ratio and Y)
use them; the selection rule below does not.

### Against a controller-independent measure: Webster's Y

R\* above is "where MaxPressure breaks down", which depends on one controller. As a check that
does not, the levels are compared with Webster's capacity limit, computed only from the flow
files and the measured saturation flows and lost time (`scripts/calibrate_indian_demand.py`,
step 4). With Webster's green splits, an intersection with critical flow ratio Y can be served
by a cycle of C seconds only if Y < (C − L) / C. At the longest cycle allowed for
`fixedtime_webster` (180 s), this gives the limit Y_cap below. It is an **ANALYTIC**
indicator, not a simulation result.

By this measure the network is much further from capacity than MaxPressure's breakdown
suggests: **peak demand uses about 55% of Webster's capacity limit, and Y only reaches the
limit at roughly 1.8 times the peak demand.** So "peak" in this project means *near the
capacity of MaxPressure as configured* (its 5 s transitions on every switch cost a third of
the time; see the next section), not near the capacity of the intersections. The results
below confirm this: the Webster plan serves the peak level without a backlog, with lower ATT
than MaxPressure.

### Why the network breaks down at a busiest-lane ratio of only ~0.25

At R* the busiest lane needs only about a quarter of its saturation flow, and Y is about 0.55
(sweep table). Classical signal timing (Webster) puts capacity at roughly
Y ≈ 1 − (share of time lost to transitions), so the early breakdown needed explaining.
`scripts/validation/diagnose_indian_breakdown.py` re-runs MaxPressure at R* with seed 2 (the
seed that failed there) and records where vehicles pile up
(`outputs/validation/indian_breakdown_5500_s2.json`):

<!-- END:breakdown -->

What the data shows:

* **About a third of every intersection's time is transition (lost) time** (30–32%,
  counted from the phase the simulator showed in each step; 32–33% under the earlier 5 s
  setting, `outputs/validation/indian_breakdown_5500_s2_maxpressure_5s.json`). MaxPressure
  switches as soon as another phase has more pressure at a decision point and min green is
  met, and every switch costs 5 s of transition. With a third lost, the usable share is about
  0.67.
* **The backlog forms at the major-road entry lanes, with no lane full.** Vehicles waiting
  to enter grow steadily through the hour, while no incoming lane reaches its storage at
  any snapshot. Almost all of the backlog is on the east and west entries going straight.
  **Spillback** (a full downstream lane blocking an upstream intersection) does not occur at
  this demand, so it is not the cause; it does appear at higher demand (e.g. 6000 veh/h and
  above), once the backlog has grown, by running the same script with `--rate 6000`.
* The remaining gap between Y ≈ 0.55 at breakdown and the ≈ 0.67 usable share is consistent
  with MaxPressure not dividing green in Webster's proportions (it reacts to queue pressure
  and uses all 8 phases, including single-approach ones). This last point is **not verified**.

This is a property of the controller as configured (frequent switches, 5 s transitions),
not just of the network, so the capacity found here is "capacity under MaxPressure", as the
calibration goal asked for. Other controllers have different capacities: the Webster plan,
which switches less, serves the peak level better (see the results and the Webster
reference above).

  to enter, and 95.6–97.7% of generated vehicles finish within the hour; the rest are still
  driving at 3600 s. MaxPressure is best (ATT 81.3 s at 15 s, 82.0 s at 5 s), then the
  Webster plan (100.4 s), then 30 s FixedTime (121.5 s).
* **At peak the Webster plan is the best baseline**, ahead of the tuned MaxPressure on every
  seed (ATT 118.3 vs 152.0, 119.6 vs 122.2 and 111.1 vs 127.4 s for seeds 0, 1, 2; mean
  116.3 vs 133.8 s), with a smaller queue and higher throughput. Both leave at most one
  vehicle waiting to enter, so both drain. The seed 1 margin is small (1.4 s).
* **Why Webster wins at peak (likely, partly verified):** at peak its cycles are longer
  (84–91 s), so it spends 23% of the time in transitions, against 32% for MaxPressure. The
  MaxPressure sweep shows the same effect within one controller: at this level the 10 s and
  20 s intervals, which also spend about 24% in transitions, reach ATT 116.2 and 115.9 s,
  the same as Webster (`docs/baselines.md`, section 1). Near the capacity of the switching
  pattern, green lost to transitions costs more than reacting to queues gains. Two
  conditions also favour Webster here: the demand is steady (Poisson arrivals at a constant
  rate, Webster's assumption) and its plan uses perfect counts of that demand. On Jinan and
  Hangzhou, where demand changes during the hour, Webster is far behind MaxPressure.
* **30 s FixedTime fails at peak; the peak level is not "beyond fixed-time capacity" in
  general.** Both 30 s plans leave about 300 vehicles (6–7%) waiting to enter, which fails the
  2% backlog criterion, and their ATT (279.9 and 291.9 s) is dominated by that backlog. The
  Webster plan, which is also fixed-time, drains. The earlier reading that the peak level
  is beyond FixedTime's capacity "by construction" holds only for an untuned 30 s plan.
* **Tuning MaxPressure helped a little** (ATT 133.8 vs 142.4 s at peak, 81.3 vs 82.0 s
  off-peak), with large seed spread at peak for both settings.
* **4-phase FixedTime beats 8-phase FixedTime** at both levels (ATT 121.5 vs 129.3 s
  off-peak, 279.9 vs 291.9 s peak), as on Jinan and Hangzhou and unlike Synthetic 4x4
  (`docs/validation.md`, section 3).
* **Seed spread is largest for MaxPressure at peak** (ATT 122.2 to 152.0 s at 15 s). Near
  its capacity, delay grows steeply with small differences in demand. Seed 0 is also the
  seed that took longest to empty in the calibration sweep at this demand (416 s vs 180 s
  and 325 s).
* **Consistency checks:** the three peak MaxPressure runs reproduce the calibration sweep's
  4750 veh/h runs exactly (same ATT, throughput and queue), and the `maxpressure_5s` runs
  reproduce the superseded 5 s runs exactly. In both cases it is the same flow files, the same
  controller and the same code path.

**For the LLM comparison this means the strongest baseline depends on the level:**
MaxPressure off-peak, the Webster plan at peak.
